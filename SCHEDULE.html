Your complete study schedule

Planning default: 17 weeks at six hours per week. There are 57 core lessons with 4,280 estimated practice minutes (71 h 20 min). The remaining planned time is for retrieval, corrections, repetition and the final demonstration. These are starting estimates, not measured promises of mastery. Three optional Later lessons add about three hours.

Your weekly rhythm

Use four 45-minute weekday slots and two 90-minute weekend slots: six hours total. Read and change examples early in the week. Spend the longer slots on independent workbook work. Reserve at least one hour each week for recall, correction and a source-change check. Split a long chapter across days; never tick it just to keep the calendar.

  1. Read the lesson and explain the example before running it.
  2. Change one input and predict the result. Compare it in real Excel.
  3. Answer the question without checking the explanation first.
  4. Complete the independent task and save your XLSX in My-work.
  5. Use the folded worked check only after attempting the task. Investigate disagreements.
  6. Reopen the workbook, explain the rule and one failure case, then tick practice. Save a JSON backup weekly.

If you fall behind

Continue the same saved workbook and lesson. Repeat the smallest failed skill, not the whole course. Add a week if needed. A quiz pass alone does not prove you can build the report. For the core finish, demonstrate a new source record flowing through query → Table → Pivot → chart, reconcile the totals, and give someone a clear handover.

The course builds spreadsheet and reporting skills, not a guaranteed analyst title, salary or job offer. Use your completed synthetic reports as portfolio evidence; keep private employer and employee data out of public uploads.
